# The ideal roster

Status: exploration, 2026-09-23. An ambitious roster designed from scratch, ignoring the current selections
and engine civs: 72 civs, each with two leaders, plus four consultation slots. The core 24 are described in
detail; the other 48 are in compact tables. It follows from [ORIGINALITY.md](ORIGINALITY.md). Abilities are design sketches, not balanced
numbers. Dates are reigns unless noted, and most early ones are approximate.

## Criteria

1. **Rulers.** Each leader headed a polity, in name or in practice. No religious founders and no one
   from the 20th century.
2. **One polity, one period.** The civ is a specific state at the leader's time, such as "Maurya", not
   "India". City names come from that period. Names that change with the era can come later.
3. **Balance across the world.** No region gets more than about a fifth of the slots.
4. **Women who ruled.** Aim for a third. Each is a head of state, or a regent who actually held power,
   never a consort.
5. **Every era.** Leaders are spread from the Bronze Age to about 1800.
6. **A distinct way to play.** Each ability comes from a documented institution or practice and does
   roughly what that institution did. Across the roster, every playstyle is covered at least twice.
7. **Evidence for the face.** The photoreal pipeline needs visual references. The *Face* column rates
   them: **strong** (made in life or close to it), **medium** (idealised or near-period) or **weak**
   (later or none, so mostly reconstruction, labelled as such).
8. **Different from Civ.** Where Civ always uses the same leader or unit, prefer an equally good
   alternative.

## Each civ

Every entry lists: the ability sketch · unique unit (UU) and unique building or improvement (UB/UI) ·
capital and cities · language for the leader's voice · notes.

### Africa

**1. New Kingdom Egypt — Hatshepsut.** *Expedition to Punt:* each new trade partner returns a luxury and
culture, and wonders build faster in cities with a trade route. · UU Medjay (scout-police) · UI Obelisk ·
Waset (Thebes), Men-nefer, Iunu, Abdju, Swenett, Khmun · Egyptian (reconstructed) · Her Deir el-Bahari
temple shows the Punt voyage. Unlike Cleopatra, she comes from a native dynasty.

**2. Aksum — Ezana.** *Coin of the Red Sea:* coastal trade routes pay extra gold, and gold minted into coin
earns faith. · UU Aksumite spearman · UB Port of Adulis · Aksum, Adulis, Matara, Yeha, Qohaito · Geʿez ·
His coins carry his bust.

**3. Mali — Mansa Musa.** *Gold of Bambuk:* gold mines give extra gold, and gold can buy great scholars.
· UU Mande bowmen · UB Manuscript library (Sankore) · Niani (location debated), Timbuktu, Gao, Djenné,
Walata · Maninka, with Arabic at court · There are no portraits. The Catalan Atlas image is symbolic,
so the face is a labelled reconstruction.

**4. Ndongo & Matamba — Njinga.** *Shifting alliances:* switching alliances has no diplomatic penalty,
and units fight better in hills and forests. · UU Kilombo warriors · UB Royal court at Kabasa ·
Kabasa, Matamba and Kindonga islands · Kimbundu · She also dealt in the slave trade. Her text must not
hide this. Depictions are European and idealised.

### West Asia

**5. Babylon — Hammurabi.** *Code of Laws:* policies cost less, and each city reduces unhappiness less.
Irrigation canals improve farms along rivers. · UU Babylonian chariot · UI Canal · Babylon, Sippar,
Kish, Borsippa, Larsa, Mari · Akkadian · The top of his law stele shows him.

**6. Achaemenid Persia — Darius I.** *Royal Road and satrapies:* roads between cities are faster, and
distant cities lose no production. · UU Sparabara (shield-bearers) · UB Satrap's seat · Parsa
(Persepolis), Pasargadae, Susa, Ecbatana, Sardis, Bactra · Old Persian · Cyrus is the alternative.

**7. Ottomans — Mehmed II.** *The Conqueror:* captured cities keep their buildings, and the capital
grows from resettlement. · UU Şahi bombard (Orban's great gun) · UB Külliye · Edirne, then Istanbul,
Bursa, Manisa, Amasya, Trabzon · Ottoman Turkish · Gentile Bellini painted him from life in 1480.

### South Asia

**8. Maurya — Ashoka.** *Edicts on stone:* after a war ends, roads carry faith and culture. Rest houses,
wells and care for people and animals speed growth. · UU Mauryan elephant corps · UB Pillar edict ·
Pataliputra, Ujjayini, Takshashila, Tosali, Suvarnagiri, Vidisha · Magadhi Prakrit · A Kanaganahalli relief
is inscribed with his name, *Rāya Asoko*.

**9. Chola — Rajendra I.** *Across the Bay:* ships carry land units early and raid coasts.
Temple towns turn faith into production. · UU Chola war fleet · UI Eri (irrigation tank) · Thanjavur,
Gangaikonda Cholapuram, Kanchipuram, Uraiyur, Nagapattinam · Tamil · His 1025 expedition reached Srivijaya.

**10. Mughal — Akbar.** *Sulh-i kul:* each religion in the empire adds happiness. Ranked officials
(mansabdari) earn gold and military strength. · UU Mughal gun elephant · UB Ibadat Khana · Agra, Fatehpur
Sikri, Lahore, Delhi, Ajmer, Ilahabad · Persian at court, Hindustani · His court painted him from life.

### East and Southeast Asia

**11. Tang–Zhou China — Wu Zetian.** *Palace examination:* great scientists and officials come faster,
and her military exam does the same for generals. · UU Tang heavy cavalry · UB Examination hall ·
Shendu (Luoyang), Chang'an, Yangzhou, Guangzhou, Chengdu, Taiyuan · Middle Chinese (reconstructed) ·
China's only reigning empress.

**12. Silla — Seondeok.** *Cheomseongdae:* an early observatory gives science and a small boost to wonders.
· UU Hwarang · UB Observatory · Seorabeol (Gyeongju) and Silla-era seats · Old Korean (sparse; see
the language note below) · The first reigning queen of Korea.

**13. Tokugawa Japan — Tokugawa Ieyasu.** *The Great Peace:* production rises in every turn of peace after
a war. Each province keeps one castle, which makes border forts cheap. · UU Ashigaru arquebusiers ·
UB Castle town (jōkamachi) · Edo, Sunpu, Kyoto, Osaka, Nagoya, Nagasaki · Early modern Japanese.

**14. Mongol Empire — Genghis Khan.** *The Yam:* relay stations give mounted units movement, sight and
healing. Units organised in tens (the decimal army) promote faster. · UU Arban riders · UB Relay station ·
Avarga, Karakorum and captured cities · Middle Mongolian · His portrait was painted about 50 years after
his death.

**15. Khmer — Jayavarman VII.** *Houses of healing:* hospitals and rest houses along roads speed growth
and healing. Reservoirs (barays) feed cities. · UU War barge (the 1177 Tonle Sap battle on the Bayon
reliefs) · UI Baray · Yasodharapura (Angkor), Phimai, Banteay Chhmar, Preah Khan of Kompong Svay ·
Old Khmer · Portrait statues survive. This is the roster's first Southeast Asian civ.

### Europe

**16. Rome — Marcus Aurelius.** *Frontier legions:* legions build roads and forts, and border forts earn
culture. · UU Auxilia · UB Castra · Roma, Carnuntum, Mediolanum, Aquileia, Lugdunum, Londinium · Latin,
with Greek for his private thoughts (he wrote the *Meditations* in Greek).

**17. Athens — Pericles.** *Delian treasury:* allied city-states pay tribute that funds culture and wonders.
· UU Trireme · UB Odeon · Athens, Piraeus, Chalcis, Eretria, Amphipolis, Thurii · Attic Greek · He was
an elected general, not a monarch. The criteria allow this, but it should be said.

**18. Franks — Charlemagne.** *Palace school:* monasteries give science, and royal envoys (missi) cut
unhappiness in distant cities. · UU Scara (heavy cavalry) · UB Palace school · Aachen, Paris, Metz,
Worms, Reims, Tours · Old Frankish and Latin · Framing it as "the Franks" leaves the French–German claim
to him alone.

**19. Tudor England — Elizabeth I.** *Sea dogs and playhouses:* privateers take gold from trade routes,
and playhouses bring great writers faster. · UU Race-built galleon · UB Playhouse · London, York,
Bristol, Norwich, Plymouth, Southampton · Early modern English.

**20. Kalmar Union — Margaret I.** *Union of crowns:* a long alliance with a city-state lets you absorb
it peacefully. · UU Royal cog · UB Royal castle · København, Roskilde, Oslo, Bergen,
Stockholm, Kalmar, Åbo · Old Danish · Her tomb effigy dates from 1423.

### Americas and Oceania

**21. Inca — Pachacuti.** *Khipu and mit'a:* labour obligations speed improvements. Terraces make hills
farmable, and roads cross mountains. · UU Chaski (runner scout) · UI Andén terrace, UB Qullqa storehouse
· Qusqu, Pisaq, Ollantaytambo, Machu Picchu, Vilcashuamán · Quechua · Portraits are colonial-era only.

**22. Mexica — Motecuhzoma I.** *Triple Alliance tribute:* conquered and allied cities pay tribute
instead of rebelling, and chinampas grow food on lakes. · UU Cuāuhtli (eagle warrior) · UI Chinampa ·
Tenochtitlan, Tlatelolco, Texcoco, Tlacopan, Coyoacan, Xochimilco · Classical Nahuatl · Use no
sacrifice mechanics.

**23. Maya — Lady Six Sky of Saal (Naranjo).** *Dynastic restoration:* each stela raised after a victory
brings a great person closer. · UU Holkan (Maya warriors) · UB Stela court · Saal, K'anwitznal (Ucanal),
Dos Pilas · Classic Mayan · Naranjo Stela 24 shows her. Pakal of Palenque is the alternative with a
stronger face.

**24. Hawaiʻi — Kamehameha I.** *Wayfinding and the Splintered Paddle:* canoes cross ocean from the start.
His 1797 law protecting non-combatants cuts unhappiness from war. · UU Peleleu war canoe ·
UI Loko iʻa (fishpond) · Kailua-Kona, Lahaina, Hilo, Kawaihae, Waikīkī · ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi · Louis Choris
drew him from life in 1816. Consult Native Hawaiian organisations.

### North America

There is no candidate here that a studio should cast alone. The strongest civ is the **Haudenosaunee
Confederacy** (the Great Law of Peace suggests an ability built around councils and alliances), but its
Grand Council has no single head. The **Powhatan paramount chiefdom** under Wahunsenacawh is the other
option. Cahokia fits a city-state better, because no ruler is named. Approach the nations first and design
with them. If that doesn't happen, leave the slot empty rather than invent a leader.

## Consultation slots

Four slots stay open until a people's own institutions take part in the design: two in North America
(the Haudenosaunee Confederacy and the Powhatan paramount chiefdom are the obvious first approaches) and
one in Aotearoa (Māori, whose iwi were never one state), and one for the Taíno of the Caribbean. Aboriginal
Australia never formed states. Don't force it into a "civ"; ask whether and how people want to be
represented at all. Budget for paid cultural advisers and credit them in the game. An empty slot is
better than an invented leader.

## Systems that make a roster this size worth it

1. **Rivals in the same era.** The roster is built in rival pairs that met in history: Rome and Carthage,
   the Mexica and Purépecha, the Inca and Chimú, Palenque and Saal, the Ottomans and Eastern Rome, Tang and
   Silla, and Mali and Songhai. Each pair gets a short scenario on a true-start map.
2. **One civ, two leaders.** The pairs above give every civ two playstyles without inventing leaders.
3. **Rising peoples.** An independent people or city-state that grows strong enough can become a playable
   civ mid-game, as the Mongols did. The roster's smaller polities (Palmyra, Malacca, Kilwa) suit this.
4. **Names that change with the era**, as ORIGINALITY.md proposes. They matter more when there are
   three Chinas and two Egypts.
5. **"What we know" cards.** Every face in the tables above has an evidence rating. Show it.
6. **Voices in each leader's own language,** with credited academic linguists for reconstructed languages.

## Sensitivity flags

Handle these civs deliberately rather than by accident:

- **Tibetan Empire:** Songtsen Gampo is revered in Tibetan Buddhism, and Tibet's status is a live
  political question with market consequences. Present the 7th-century empire factually; decide early
  whether to ship it.
- **Abbasids:** there are no portraits, and depicting a caliph matters to some players. Consider a
  voice and a setting without a photoreal face, or an evidence-led stylised portrait.
- **Castile:** Isabella's reign includes the Inquisition, the 1492 expulsion of the Jews and the start
  of colonisation in the Americas. Her text must not celebrate them. The alternative is to leave her
  on the bench.
- **Benin:** the Benin Bronzes are at the centre of a restitution debate. Consult the Oba's court and
  Edo scholars before using that imagery.
- **Timur, Genghis Khan and Ashurbanipal:** mass killing is documented. Don't glorify it.
- **Njinga, Kongo, Kilwa and Songhai:** each was involved in the slave trade. Tell that plainly.
- **Haiti:** Toussaint governed a colony rather than an independent state, and fits only the "in
  practice" test. Dessalines led the independent state.
- **Bench, not roster:** Kyivan Rus' and Muscovy, because of the Russia–Ukraine war; the Marathas
  (Shivaji), who are a live political symbol in India; and Asante, whose Golden Stool is sacred today.
  Each can join after consultation.

- **Eras:** 4 before 1200 BCE, 17 from 1200 BCE to 600 CE, 25 from 600 to 1400, and 26 from 1400 to 1820.
- **Women:** 16 of 72 first leaders (22%) and 8 more among the second leaders, so 24 of 144. That misses
  the one-third aim. In many polities, reigning women are hard to document. The honest route is more
  women-led polities, not weaker picks: Jindeok of Silla, Empress Suiko (Asuka Japan), Christina of
  Sweden, Maria Theresa of Austria and Catherine II (on the bench while Russia is).
- **Faces:** 11 strong, 27 medium, 34 weak. Nearly half are reconstructions, so the "What we know" card
  is a requirement, not polish.

## Bench

Good civs that did not make the 72: Asante (Osei Tutu), Mutapa, Kanem-Bornu (Idris Alooma), the
Marathas (Shivaji), the Sikh Empire (Ranjit Singh), Kyivan Rus' and Muscovy, Venice, Wessex (Alfred),
Gaul (Vercingetorix), Yuan (Kublai Khan), Asuka Japan (Suiko), the Xiongnu (Modu Chanyu), Srivijaya,
the Trưng sisters' rising (Trưng Trắc), Muisca and Nabataea (Aretas IV). The sensitivity flags explain
why several are held back.

## Language note

Several voices would be reconstructions: Egyptian, Old Korean, Middle Chinese, Classic Mayan, Old Persian and
Akkadian. Choose one rule for all of them. Either use a scholarly reconstruction and say so on the card, or
use the nearest living descendant (Coptic, modern Korean, and so on), with subtitles either way.
